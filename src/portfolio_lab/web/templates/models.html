Models

Instead of one hand-built score, these models learn how to combine every stock trait (and, for the tree model, the market environment) into a probability that the stock beats the median stock over the next month or quarter. Each January a model is trained only on earlier months, then judged on the year it never saw.

Reading this

  • AUC: the chance that, of two stocks, the model rates higher the one that then did better. 0.5 is a coin flip; at monthly horizons 0.52 is a useful edge, because it repeats across thousands of stocks.
  • Top / bottom tenth: each month, how often the tenth of stocks rated most likely to beat the median did so, and how often the least likely tenth lagged. This is what a portfolio would hold or avoid, and does not depend on calibration.
  • Calibration error: the average gap between stated and realized probabilities; the arrow shows it after calibration (re-mapped using only earlier years' outcomes). Honest probabilities for single stocks sit close to 50%: the reliability comes from breadth, many small edges at once, not from certainty about any one stock.
  • Models are named in the glossary; their monthly IC is also on the scoreboard.
