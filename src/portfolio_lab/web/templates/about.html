How it works

A personal research lab for stock portfolio strategies. It started as a crude "value investing" optimizer (Piotroski F-score filter + efficient frontier); this version rebuilds it on a foundation that can say whether any strategy actually works.

1. Universe

Every common stock and ADR listed on NASDAQ, NYSE and NYSE American, from NASDAQ's official symbol directory, refreshed daily. ETFs, funds, warrants, units, rights, preferred shares and notes are excluded. On any given day a stock is only eligible to hold if, using data up to that day, it closed above $5, traded a median of over $1M a day for the past 60 days, and has at least a year of history.

2. Prices

Daily bars since 2016 from Alpaca's market data (the full consolidated tape), updated every trading evening after 8pm Eastern. Each day stores the prices that actually traded, plus returns adjusted for splits and dividends, so a stock's history never changes when it later splits.

3. Backtests

  • Walk forward, no peeking. At each rebalance a strategy sees data only up to that day's close. A test re-runs every strategy with all future data scrambled and requires its decisions to be identical.
  • Realistic execution. Trades happen at the next day's open. Costs are charged for crossing the spread plus market impact that grows with trade size relative to a stock's volume (at a $100k portfolio). Uninvested cash earns the 3-month Treasury bill rate.
  • Honest comparisons. Every strategy is scored against buy-and-hold SPY and an equal-weight portfolio of all eligible stocks: return, volatility, Sharpe ratio, drawdown, and alpha, beta and information ratio versus SPY.

4. Strategies

  • Baselines: buy-and-hold SPY, and equal weight (all eligible stocks, or the most liquid ones).
  • Mean-variance: the original optimizer's idea done properly. Return forecasts (the legacy ARIMA on prices, an AR(1) model on returns, or a plain trailing average) feed the efficient frontier over the 100 most liquid stocks.
  • Momentum: equal-weight the 20 biggest winners of the past year (skipping the last month), on the same 100 stocks or on every eligible stock.

What the comparison shows so far. The forecasts add nothing over a plain trailing average. Plain momentum on the same 100 stocks earns the same return as mean-variance (about 25% a year since 2017), so the return is momentum; what the optimizer adds is risk control, with market-level beta and drawdowns instead of momentum's 1.35 beta and -50% drawdown, for a much better Sharpe ratio. Momentum across all eligible stocks does badly, so the effect lived in large, liquid names. All of this is flattered by survivorship bias, so treat it as an upper bound.

  • Next: a Piotroski F-score value filter computed from SEC filings as of the date each filing was public.

Roadmap

  1. Foundation (this site): data, backtests, baselines, and the original optimizer rebuilt.
  2. Forecasting: attention-based sequence models using market-wide inputs, judged against the same baselines.
  3. Lead-lag network: do some stocks consistently react later than others to market leaders? Scan every pair for delayed reactions and test whether they persist out of sample.

Caveats

  • Survivorship bias: free data covers stocks listed today, so companies that went bankrupt or were delisted are missing. That flatters results, especially for small caps.
  • Trading costs are a model, not real fills.
  • This is research, not investment advice.

Built with

Python with polars, NumPy and PyPortfolioOpt; FastAPI, HTMX and Plotly for this site. It runs on an Orange Pi 5 at home. Source: github.com/nick-fournier/portfolio-lab.
