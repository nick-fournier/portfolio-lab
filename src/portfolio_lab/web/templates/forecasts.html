How it works

What it predicts. Each stock's return over the next month minus that month's average return across all stocks:

ri=Ri−R‾

The market's own rise or fall is the same for every stock, so it can't help rank them and is left out. Every number on this page is in these units.

Inputs, as known at each month end:

  • 103 stock inputs: valuation, profitability, growth, investment, momentum, volatility, liquidity and more, each as the stock's percentile among that month's stocks.
  • 116 market inputs: interest rates, credit spreads, oil, jobs, inflation, the VIX and the market's own state. They are the same for every stock in a month, so they can only matter by changing how the stock inputs pay off.

Two models, averaged:

  1. Linear + trees. A straight-line model on the stock inputs and on each input × the VIX and × last month's spread of stock returns, then boosted trees on what it misses (stock and market inputs), added at a strength chosen from earlier months (0.25).
  2. Nets. A small neural network turns each stock's 103 inputs into 8 numbers; the market inputs set the weights on those numbers each month. A penalty keeps the market's effect small unless the data clearly support it.
forecast=12(linear + trees)+12nets

The two make different mistakes (their forecasts correlate {{ "%.2f" | format(s.corr) }}), so the average ranks better than either. Linear + trees gets the size of the biggest winners and losers about right; the nets rank more steadily.

Limits

  • A year's IC moves by about ±{{ "%.3f" | format(s.yearly_noise) }} by chance alone at this level of skill, as much as the signal itself: good and bad years are mostly luck, not patterns the model misses.
  • Forecasts are too big in weak years and about right in strong ones (slope {{ "%.2f" | format(f.slope) }} overall); their size doesn't widen when markets get rougher. Rescaling them from earlier months' skill made the fit worse, so they are left as they are.
  • More gains likely need new information, such as trade policy or foreign rates (#54).
