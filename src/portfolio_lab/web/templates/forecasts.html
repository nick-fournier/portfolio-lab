How it works

What it predicts. Each stock's return from one month end to the next, minus that month's T-bill: what the stock is expected to earn over cash. Class 2 hands it to the optimizer as each stock's expected return; class 1 (production) uses the stock's trailing one-year return instead.

Nine terms, as known at each month end:

  • Momentum: the stock's return over the last year and over the last six months.
  • Cash and value: operating cash flow over assets, and the free-cash-flow, sales, earnings and R&D-to-market yields.
  • Market trend: each momentum times SPY's return over the last year, so momentum can count for more or less depending on how the market has been doing.

Each stock input is put on one scale per month: divided by the month's typical size, squashed with asinh (which tames extreme values but keeps the sign) and standardized across that month's stocks. A missing input counts as average.

forecast=a+∑k=17bkxk+c1mom12·SPY12+c2mom6·SPY12

Fitting. Ordinary least squares on every stock (price at least $1, at least $100k traded a day) in every earlier month, about 3,500 a month, refit every night. No trees, no nets, no penalties: a straight line on terms that each earned their place one at a time.

Into the optimizer: Grinold's rule. A forecast is mostly noise, so class 2 doesn't use its size directly. Among the candidates it standardizes the forecasts (z) and sizes them by the forecast's own track record: the T-bill plus √12 · IC · volatility · z, where IC is the average monthly rank correlation between forecasts and outcomes in every earlier month. These are honest expected returns. How hard to bet on them is set separately: the optimizer (Kelly) bets 16 times as hard as full Kelly, a choice measured on past returns (risk aversion 1/16 did best, and so did a month-by-month pick on earlier data only).

Limits

  • A year's IC moves by about ±{{ "%.3f" | format(s.yearly_noise) }} by chance alone: good and bad years are mostly luck.
  • As fitted, the forecasts are too big (slope {{ "%.2f" | format(f.slope) }}); class 2 uses their ranking and takes their size from Grinold's rule instead (chart above).
  • The nine terms were chosen on the same 2004–2026 history they are graded on, one at a time and only if they helped in both halves of it; anything chosen that way looks a little better here than it will in the future.
