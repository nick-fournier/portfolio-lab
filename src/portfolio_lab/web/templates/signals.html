Signal scoreboard

Does a model actually predict returns? Each signal predicts a fixed time ahead (a month, a week or a day). At the end of every such period it ranks the stocks, using only data up to that day, and the ranking is compared with the returns over the next period. This tests the prediction on its own, before any optimizer, costs or position limits get involved.

Reading this

  • Mean IC is the average per-period rank correlation between a signal's scores and the returns that followed. 0 means no skill; 0.02 to 0.05 is typical of signals worth using, because a small edge repeats across many stocks and months.
  • t-stat is the mean IC over its standard error. Beyond about ±2 (colored) it is unlikely to be luck.
  • Top / bottom fifth are annualized returns of the highest- and lowest-scored fifth of stocks, equally weighted, before costs. IC compares rankings while these compare averages, so they can disagree: a few huge winners among volatile stocks lift the average of a group whose typical member lost.
  • In the cumulative chart, a steady climb means persistent skill; a flat or wandering line means none.
