Signal scoreboard

Does a model actually predict returns? At the end of every month each signal ranks the stocks, using only data up to that day, and the ranking is compared with the returns of the next {{ horizon }} trading days. This tests the prediction on its own, before any optimizer, costs or position limits get involved.

What is IC?

The information coefficient measures how well a signal's ranking of stocks matched the ranking of what they actually returned next. For each month:

  1. Rank the stocks in the pool by the signal's score (1 = highest predicted return).
  2. Rank the same stocks by the return they actually earned over the next month.
  3. The IC is the correlation between the two rankings (Spearman rank correlation).

+1 means the order was predicted perfectly, 0 means no better than shuffling, −1 means exactly backwards. Only the order matters, so one stock that doubles cannot dominate. Each month gives one noisy IC; the table averages them over every month. Useful signals typically average 0.02 to 0.05: small, but repeated across hundreds of stocks and many months.

Reading this

  • Mean IC is the average IC over every month (see above).
  • t-stat is the mean IC over its standard error. Beyond about ±2 (colored) it is unlikely to be luck.
  • Top / bottom fifth are annualized returns of the highest- and lowest-scored fifth of stocks, equally weighted, before costs. IC compares rankings while these compare averages, so they can disagree: a few huge winners among volatile stocks lift the average of a group whose typical member lost.
  • In the cumulative chart, a steady climb means persistent skill; a flat or wandering line means none.
